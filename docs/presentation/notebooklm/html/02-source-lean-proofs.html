The Lean Proofs: From Raw Tape to a Single Conjecture

Two machines, one architecture

The project's formal work centers on two six-state, two-symbol Turing machines from the BB(6) open set, written in the community's standard text format:

  • Antihydra: 1RB1RA_0LC1LE_1LD1LC_1LA0LB_1LF1RE_---0RA — the most famous BB(6) cryptid.
  • Dossier-1: 1RB0LE_1LB1RC_0RF0RD_1RE0LA_1LA1RE_---0RB — an unsolved holdout with a richer, stack-like structure.

Both developments live in Lean 4, deliberately restricted to core Lean: no Mathlib, no automation beyond the built-in omega arithmetic solver, and a mechanically enforced axiom budget of exactly two universally trusted axioms, propext and Quot.sound. The point of the austerity is auditability: a reviewer can read the entire trust base — the transition table, the tape, the step function — in minutes.

Layer one: raw semantics and halt localization

A configuration is a control state, an integer head position, and a tape modeled as a total function from integers to booleans. One step consults the transition table; the single undefined entry (state F reading 0) is the only way to halt. Three foundational theorems per machine:

  1. The machine halts in one step exactly at that undefined transition.
  2. State F is only ever entered from one specific transition — so any halt is preceded by a specific local tape pattern.
  3. Halt localization: any halting run must pass through a reachable "probe" — the danger state reading a 0 whose critical neighbor is also 0.

This yields the safety contract: if every 0 the danger state ever reads has a 1 on the critical side, the machine can never halt.

Layer two: the collapse dynamics, proved rule by rule

Watch either machine at its records — the moments it reaches fresh tape — and the tape is always solid blocks of ones separated by single zeros. The block lengths evolve by exact arithmetic rules, and each rule became a theorem giving the exact raw trajectory for all block values, along with a safety certificate that no halt can occur during the rule.

For Antihydra there are three rules acting on triples (a, m, c): the hydra rule (m ≥ 3) runs exactly 2c + 11 raw steps and sends (a, m, c) to (a, m − 2, c + 3) with the counter a untouched — about 96 percent of all dynamics; the drop (m = 2) costs the counter one unit; the merge (m = 1) feeds it two. The abstract orbit iterates these from (2, 7, 2), reached from the blank tape by a proven 62-step startup. The orbit dies — and only then can the machine possibly halt — exactly when a drop fires with the counter at 1 or less. The conjecture SafeOrbitA says the orbit never dies. The main theorem: SafeOrbitA implies Antihydra never halts from the blank tape — machine-checked, no sorry, two axioms.

Dossier-1 is the same architecture with a deeper abstract state: a stack of chain levels (a list of numbers) plus an active pair, with seven proven transport rules including genuine collapse cascades where the last level folds into its parent. Its conjecture SafeOrbit encodes a recursive Collatz checkpoint law (odd n maps to (3n+7)/2). Main theorem: SafeOrbit implies dossier-1 never halts from the blank tape.

The bridge to the community's conjecture

The community states the Antihydra conjecture one-dimensionally: iterate b → b + ⌊b/2⌋ from 8, keep a heads counter that gains 2 on even values and loses 1 on odd values, and the machine halts if the counter would go negative. A bridge theorem proves a step-for-step conjugacy between the project's triple orbit and this 1-D process: the hidden change of variables is b = m + 2(c−2)/3 + 5, the machine's counter block is the heads counter, and the parities line up exactly — merges are the even steps, drops are the odd ones. The proof runs the two processes in lock-step and shows deaths coincide.

One honest delta is documented rather than hidden: the formal tape grammar needs a non-empty counter block, so the formal orbit stops tracking the machine one drop earlier than the community's process — death at counter ≤ 1 instead of exactly 0, and the sequence starts at 12 = H(8) because the proven startup absorbs the first term. So the formal hypothesis is possibly stronger than the wiki's conjecture by one boundary case, listed as open follow-up work.

Trust engineering: parity, parsing, and axiom hunting

Three supporting layers make the trust story checkable rather than takeable on faith:

  • Parser parity. The hand-typed transition tables were the one trust-critical transcription. A total parser from the standard text format, written in Lean, is proved by kernel computation to produce exactly the hand-written tables — with corrupted-code negative controls so the theorem cannot pass vacuously.
  • Differential testing. The proved step function was made executable and byte-compared, step by step for thousands of steps, against an independent thirty-line Python simulator. The traces agree exactly, and the proven startup milestones appear at the right steps.
  • Axiom hunting. Two subtle contamination channels were discovered and engineered around: Lean's omega tactic quietly uses the classical choice axiom when asked to prove a positive "and"/"or" goal (feed it only atomic goals and it stays clean), and the innocuous String.toList function drags classical choice into any proof that touches it (certify over character lists instead). Every headline theorem reports exactly propext and Quot.sound, verified by a gate script on every build.

What is and is not claimed

Proven: every dynamical statement about both machines, the reductions, the bridge, the parser parity. Assumed: the two orbit conjectures — one of which is the famous Antihydra conjecture, believed true on random-walk grounds and proven by no one. Decided: nothing. The machines' fates remain open; what changed is that each fate now is a clean arithmetic statement.
