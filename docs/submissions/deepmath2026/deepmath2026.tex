\begin{abstract}
The busy beaver value $BB(n)$ is the longest finite runtime of any
$n$-state Turing machine started on a blank tape. $BB(5)$ was settled
in 2024 with a computer-checked proof. $BB(6)$ is blocked by a handful
of machines whose tapes secretly run Collatz-style arithmetic; nobody
can prove whether they stop. This paper studies two of these
machines and surveys the rest. The approach is empirical first: the
machine's behavior is logged as a
sequence of events, the event data embedded in logarithmic, simplex, and
hyperbolic coordinates, and the dynamics found to be piecewise
affine in a five-integer state. The description is then proven
correct in the Lean proof assistant, down to the machine's individual steps and
with a minimal axiom footprint. The result is a machine-checked
theorem: if a simple five-integer recurrence never enters an explicit
finite set of bad configurations, the machine never halts. The
converse pressure is also proven: entering a bad configuration forces the
machine to halt. The open problem is thereby moved, without loss, from
a Turing machine to a short arithmetic recurrence. The renewal map of the recurrence is then derived in closed form and shown
to have a width-five fatal window around the diagonal, and the two mechanical
proof routes with computational evidence: no congruence invariant up to
modulus 384 excludes the window, and the branch dynamics has
exponential factor complexity, so it is not conjugate to a rotation.
Measuring the recurrence over its whole seed space shows the set of
dying seeds has natural density zero at the Borel--Cantelli rate
($\approx 12/X$), so the studied machines' seeds are typical
survivors.
Surveying the entire holdout set with a two-ended harness (forward
orbit against backward halt cone, regimes fitted in between)
classifies all $1{,}094$ open six-state machines: zero halts, zero
alignments; four fixed-dimension machines, exactly the certified
ones; an eleven-machine \emph{array class} whose state spaces grow in
dimension, for whose leading member an exact word-rewrite calculus
is constructed (a two-phase odometer with a block-trisection boundary rule)
validated against $2 \times 10^{8}$ raw steps; and $307$ single-law
machines among $346$ with regime structure. The program is framed
on a verification spectrum whose proven endpoint is
$\mathsf{MIP}^{*} = \mathsf{RE}$: kernel checking at the bottom,
certified predictive emulation (this paper) in the middle, and
entangled-prover verification of halting-type claims as the goal. Every
empirical claim is validated against $10^{9}$ raw machine steps, and
the recurrence is tested for $10^{8}$ iterations. For the theory of
learning, the pipeline is representation learning carried to a
proof, and it is offered as a benchmark: unbounded, exactly labeled,
law-governed sequence data on which a learned representation has a
provable ground truth, with a two-prover game that verifies the
forecasts of any learned emulator at constant cost per check.
\end{abstract}

\section{The problem, from zero}
A Turing machine here is a finite table: six control states, a tape
of 0s and 1s, and for each (state, read symbol) pair an instruction
(write a bit, move left or right, change state) or nothing. A
machine with no instruction for its current situation halts.
$BB(n)$ is the longest runtime among the $n$-state tables that
eventually halt from the all-zero tape. The function is
noncomputable \cite{aaronson,michel}, so each value is won machine
by machine: every table either halts (run it) or must be
\emph{proven} to run forever.

The bbchallenge collaboration settled $BB(5)=47{,}176{,}870$ in the
Rocq proof assistant, ``the first determination of a new Busy Beaver
value in over 40 years'' \cite{coqbb5,deciders}. At six states a
residual set of machines defeats all known automated deciders; the
community calls them cryptids. They are hard for a concrete reason:
their tapes implement iterated arithmetic of the same character as
the Collatz ($3x{+}1$) map, where the strongest known result, Tao's
theorem that ``$\mathrm{Col}_{\min}(N) \le f(N)$ for almost all
$N$'' \cite{tao-collatz}, concerns typical orbits, while busy
beaver questions need one specific orbit followed forever.

The subject here is a mirror-image pair of six-state cryptids,
called dossier-2 and dossier-3. Machine one, written
\texttt{1RB1RC\_1LC1LC\_1RE0LD\_1LB1RF\_---1RA\_0RD1RD} in the standard
notation, is the following table. Each entry gives (bit written, head
move, next state); the machine starts in state $A$ on an all-zero tape.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{c|cc}
state & reads 0 & reads 1 \\ \hline
$A$ & 1, R, $B$ & 1, R, $C$ \\
$B$ & 1, L, $C$ & 1, L, $C$ \\
$C$ & 1, R, $E$ & 0, L, $D$ \\
$D$ & 1, L, $B$ & 1, R, $F$ \\
$E$ & \textbf{(none: halt)} & 1, R, $A$ \\
$F$ & 0, R, $D$ & 1, R, $D$ \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The single empty entry is the only way to halt: the machine must be in
state $E$ reading a 0. Working backward through the table, state $E$
is only entered from state $C$ reading a 0, so any halt must pass
through the configuration ``$C$ reads 0 with another 0 to its right.''
Call this the \emph{probe event}. The reader should hold on to
this configuration: every theorem below is, in the end, a statement
about it, and the whole paper is one long argument that the probe
event never happens.

\section{Method}
The method has three stages: watch, compress, certify. One
principle recurs throughout. Every empirical discovery must
eventually be either proven exactly or discarded; nothing in the
pipeline is permitted to remain approximately true.

\textbf{Watch.} Run the machine and record only the moments that
matter. For these machines there is a natural clock: a recurring
configuration called a spawn entry. Between two spawn entries the
machine performs one bounded ``episode'' of work. Sampling at spawn
entries turns a raw trajectory of $10^{9}$ steps into a clean sequence
of a few million events.

\textbf{Compress.} At every spawn entry the tape has the same form: a
solid block of 1s with exactly two 0-cells (``holes'') inside it, the
head at the block's right end. A concrete instant of the certified
orbit (machine at position $r$, dots are 0s):
$$\cdots 0\,0\,\underbrace{1\,1\,1\,1\,1\,1}_{a}\,
\underset{h_1}{0}\,\underbrace{1\,1\,1\,1}_{b-1}\,
\underset{h_2}{0}\,\underbrace{1\,1\,1\,1\,1\,1\,1}_{c-1}\,
\underset{r}{\downarrow}\,0\,0 \cdots$$
So the whole configuration is five integers: the block's endpoints
$l, r$, the hole positions $h_1, h_2$, and a tag; write
$a = h_1 - l$, $b = h_2 - h_1$, $c = r - h_2$ for the gaps. Between
spawn entries the machine performs one of seven ``episodes,'' each
later certified with its exact step count:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lll}
episode & steps & effect on the tape \\ \hline
spawn & $7$ & extend block by 1, dig a fresh hole at $r-2$ \\
crawl & $10$ per cycle & the walking gait; moves 2 cells, carries a hole \\
cross & $13$ & absorb a hole, re-dig it 4 cells right \\
terminate & $13$ & fill a hole, plant it 6 right (the probe events) \\
fuse & $19$ & merge adjacent holes, plant 9 right \\
edge & $10$ or $13$ & extend the left end by 1 or 3 (renewal) \\
sweep & $2$ per cell & return home; holes cross for free \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

For instance, the crawl is a fixed ten-step loop: the machine marches
left three cells twice (writing $1,1,0$ each time), then backs up four;
the net effect is two cells of travel and a 0 that the walker carries
along three cells behind itself. The danger state $C$ reads a 0
\emph{only} inside terminate and fuse episodes, and there the theorems
show the cell to its right always holds a 1, so the probe event cannot
fire. Plotted in raw coordinates, the event data looks like growth
curves with no visible law (Figure~\ref{fig:std}). Plotted in the
right coordinates the same data is piecewise linear: componentwise
$\log(1{+}x)$ straightens each regime, the projective simplex
$(a,b,c)/(a{+}b{+}c)$ removes scale, and hyperbolic and spherical
renderings of those two charts expose the orbit's nested renewal
structure (Figure~\ref{fig:charts}). Straight segments
are one regime; corners are regime changes.

These coordinate choices are quantum-inspired data science in a
precise, bounded sense, and each has published lineage. The log
chart is a dequantization: in Viro's words, ``on logarithmic paper
some real algebraic curves look like smoothed broken lines''
\cite{viro}, and Litvinov states the limit exactly: ``tropical
mathematics can be treated as a result of the so-called Maslov
dequantization of the traditional mathematics over numerical fields
as the Planck constant $\hbar$ tends to zero taking imaginary
values'' \cite{litvinov}. The piecewise-linear regimes here are that
classical limit, observed in a Turing machine's arithmetic. The
simplex chart with its Fisher--Rao metric is the classical face of
quantum state geometry, made literal by the amplitude map
$p \mapsto \sqrt{p}$ onto the positive octant of the sphere. The
hyperbolic chart follows the machine-learning use of negative
curvature for hierarchies, which ``allows us to learn parsimonious
representations of symbolic data by simultaneously capturing
hierarchy and similarity'' \cite{nickel-kiela}; the nested renewal
generations are exactly such a hierarchy. Two further disciplines
carried over from quantum simulation practice: the vector encodings
of Collatz-like steps are \emph{reversible} maps (the circuit-model
requirement that every operation be invertible), which is what makes
backward orbit search, the classical analogue of uncomputation,
available for the halt cones of the census below; and candidate
states are evolved in ensembles. The boundary is stated plainly: no
quantum computation is performed anywhere in this pipeline, and no
quantum advantage is claimed; ``quantum-inspired'' names the origin
of the geometries and the disciplines, not a resource. The
underlying view of a Turing machine as a dynamical system goes back
to Moore, for whose generalized shifts ``many simple questions about
their dynamics are undecidable'' \cite{moore}, and to K\r{u}rka
\cite{kurka}.

\textbf{Certify.} The picture suggests a law; the law must then be
proven about the actual machine, or it is only a picture. It is
first validated empirically and fail-closed: a predictor that knows the
conjectured law must reproduce every event, with exact positions and exact
tape, over $10^{9}$ raw steps per machine, and any miss aborts. It
reproduces all of them. The law is then proven in Lean~4, using only the
kernel's minimal axioms (\textsf{propext}, \textsf{Quot.sound}; no
classical choice). The certified account consists of: seven theorems,
one per episode type, each giving the episode's exact step count,
exact output shape, and safety; two inductions that handle the
episodes' unbounded middle parts; a concrete certificate for the
machine's startup (235 steps for one machine, 2{,}044 for the other);
and a transport theorem showing that a five-integer function
$\mathsf{metaStep}$, iterated from the startup state, predicts the
machine forever.

\section{The result}
Halting, for these machines, can only happen one way: a specific
control state must read a 0 with another 0 beside it. The certified
episode theorems show this situation never arises while the
five-integer iteration stays inside an explicit guarded region.
Writing $\Safe$ for the statement that the iteration never leaves
that region, and $\Halts(\mathsf{blankStart})$ for the statement
that the machine halts from the all-zero tape:

\begin{theorem}
For each machine, checked by the Lean kernel at axioms
$\{\mathrm{propext},\mathrm{Quot.sound}\}$:
$\ \Safe \rightarrow \lnot\Halts(\mathsf{blankStart})$.
\end{theorem}

Two facts make this reduction meaningful, not cosmetic. First,
it is complete: startup, one exceptional (individually certified)
episode, and every future
step of the machine are covered by theorems, so nothing about the
machine remains to be understood except the arithmetic. Second, it is
sharp: the configurations just outside the guarded region were
constructed, and the machine run from them; it halts within about fifty
steps. Leaving the region is not merely unproven-safe. It is fatal.
So $\Safe$ \emph{is} the halting problem, restated as arithmetic.

The arithmetic is explicit. Inside the main regime the iteration is
$(a,b,c) \mapsto (a{+}6,\,b{-}2,\,c{-}2)$, so $\gamma = c - b$ is
conserved while the counters drain. When $b$ or $c$ runs out, a
renewal move re-seeds the triple by subtracting and swapping counters,
with parity choosing the branch: part Euclidean algorithm, part
Collatz. Figure~\ref{fig:gamma} shows the renewal process as its own
dynamical system: the return map of $\gamma$, with the fatal values
$\gamma \in \{1,3,5\}$ marked. The recurrence avoids the band for $10^{8}$
iterations (about $10^{15}$ machine steps). Proving it does so forever
is the open problem, stated entirely in elementary arithmetic.

The problem admits a further characterization. Sampling the orbit at its
renewal section (a Poincar\'e section of the dynamics) reduces the
whole dynamics to a two-counter map,
verified exactly against the certified orbit: with
$(\alpha,\beta) = (6, a{-}1)$ or $(9, a{-}2)$ by the parity of $a$,
$$R(a,b) = \begin{cases}
(a+6,\ b-3) & b \text{ even},\\
(\alpha + 3(b-3),\ \beta - (b-3)) & b \text{ odd},\ b < \beta,\\
(\alpha + 3\beta + 3,\ b - \beta - 4) & b \text{ odd},\ b > \beta.
\end{cases}$$
The case $b = \beta$ never occurs on the verified orbit, against
which the map is checked transition by transition.
Its fatal set is geometrically simple: death occurs exactly when the
two counters arrive almost equal ($b - \beta \in \{1,3,5\}$, plus
two boundary cases). Two mechanical proof routes are then closed by
computation. A sound residue-abstraction search finds a genuine mod-3
conservation law but shows that no congruence invariant up to modulus
$384$ excludes the fatal window. And the branch-symbol sequence has
factor complexity growing like $1.6^{n}$, where a rotation coding
would give $n+1$, so the dynamics is not conjugate to a rigid system, and
continued-fraction rigidity arguments do not apply as stated. Over
$10^{5}$ renewals the orbit's minimum distance above the fatal window
is $4$: it grazes death and survives, so no proof can rely on a
structural buffer. What remains is a pointwise avoidance statement for
a pseudorandom multiplicative map, the Collatz situation, with
three mitigations: the map is explicit and total, the fatal set is a
finite union of lines near the diagonal, and the certified reduction
stands under any future argument. The natural next language is
hyperbolic: renewal steps are integer $2{\times}2$ matrix moves, and a
near-tie of the counters is a cusp excursion on the modular surface, so
the conjecture asks that a specific geodesic avoid three explicit
horoballs forever \cite{series}.

\emph{Almost all seeds survive.} The map $R$ was also measured over
its whole seed space, not just the certified orbit. Enumerating every seed in
$[X, 2X)^2$ exactly for $X \le 256$, and sampling to $X = 2^{16}$,
the fraction of seeds whose orbit ever dies is $\approx 12/X$: the
dying set has natural density zero, at the rate a Borel--Cantelli
heuristic predicts. The mechanism is visible in the data. Every
observed death occurs within the first fifty renewals; the offset
$b - \beta$ at the diagonal is uniformly distributed, fatal values
included; and sizes grow by a factor $e^{0.134}$ per renewal, so the
per-renewal hazard $\approx 6/\mathrm{size}$ sums geometrically:
death is an early-game phenomenon, and survival compounds. The
studied machines' seeds are therefore \emph{typical survivors}, not
exceptions. (One measurement artifact is recorded here. An early
instrument logged offsets only on steps actually taken and reported
zero fatal offsets in $2 \times 10^5$ events; this is survivorship
bias in textbook form, since a fatal offset is a death and never generates
a step. Corrected instrumentation restored exact agreement with the
uniform prediction.) A rigorous almost-all theorem needs one missing
piece: the tail-hazard bound along orbits, an invariant-measure
argument in the style of Tao's Collatz result, with the advantage
that $R$ is explicit, total, piecewise affine, and its hazard is
measured uniform.

\section{The census: what the remaining 1{,}094 machines are}
The pipeline above certifies individual machines. Run in survey
form across the entire BB(6) holdout set, it yields a
classification: to the authors' knowledge, the first structural census of
everything that remains open at six states.

The instrument is a three-part harness modeled on a two-prover
frame (whose provenance the next section makes precise): a
\emph{start machine} (forward
emulation from the blank tape), an \emph{end machine} (the backward
halt cone: every partial configuration that could precede a halt
within $k$ steps), and a \emph{regime machine} that detects and fits
the piecewise laws a run moves through, using second differences of
extreme-event times. A machine halts if and only if its forward orbit
ever aligns with its backward cone, so the verdict compiles from the
three reports. Across all $1{,}094$ holdouts: zero halts, and zero
alignments; at every sampled instant, every holdout is at least
$18$ raw steps from any halting event. (For dossier-3 the two ends
are provably incompatible in a stronger sense: all $134$ backward
families at depth $24$ require three $0$s within a six-cell window,
a pattern no certified forward shape can produce; the statement is
finite and checkable.) The census then splits the set:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{4 machines} reduce to
  \emph{fixed-dimension} integer maps, a bounded tuple of counters
  with certified episode theorems: the pair of this paper, an
  earlier machine of ours (dossier-1), and Antihydra, the
  community's best-known cryptid. These are the machines the previous
  sections certify, and the sweep found no others: ranked by regime
  cleanliness, the top $25$ candidates yield $11$ machines with exact
  fail-closed orbit specifications, and \emph{every one of the eleven}
  fails both fixed-shape gates (bounded hole count, bounded
  run-length dimension). The certified four are, up to this ranking's
  reach, the only fixed-dimension machines in the cohort.
\item \textbf{The array class.} Those $11$ exact-but-unbounded
  machines are the new frontier: their tape shapes are growing
  \emph{vectors} of blocks; the state space itself gains a
  dimension every few regimes. Their orbit specifications are exact
  but fragment into hundreds of rules, each witnessed by only one
  or two observed transitions; the law
  is real but lives on a space of growing dimension.
\item \textbf{307 machines} are governed by a single affine law in
  the regime harness (the structured third of the cohort, $346$ with
  detectable regime structure); the rest show no structure at the
  probed horizons.
\end{itemize}

For the top array-class machine
(\texttt{1RB1RD\_1LC0LC\_0RD0LB\_1RF0LE\_0RD---\_0RA1RC}) the
array-class analogue of the episode calculus of the previous
sections was then built: an exact
\emph{word-rewrite system}. Its tape at each canonical event is a
word of $1$-blocks of lengths $1$, $2$, $3$ with single-$0$ gaps; a
sweep of the head is a sequential transducer over that word, and the
whole dynamics compresses to five interior rules (copy, absorb,
cursor, insert, and one parametric regroup family with cost $8+6k$),
two parametric boundary families, one of which is a literal
\emph{block trisection} ($3 \mapsto 1{,}1{,}1$), and two derived
laws: a deposited $2$-block acts as a \emph{cursor}, restarting the
next pass at its own position, and whether a pass ends in a restart
is read off the output word. Semantically the machine is a two-phase
odometer on $\{1,3\}$-words: the $3$s are carries, the cursor is the
carry boundary. The system is validated the way the certified
machines were before their Lean compilation. A \emph{segment} is
one full pass of the head between successive returns to the growing
end of the tape; the words at those returns are the states of the
rewrite system, and validation is out-of-sample: the rules are
frozen after a training prefix and must reproduce every later
segment. Rules learned on
$4 \times 10^{7}$ raw steps reproduce $2{,}179$ of $2{,}186$
held-out segments over the next $1.6 \times 10^{8}$ steps
\emph{exactly} (output word, arrival type, step count, head
position), with zero chaining violations; every exception is a
flagged boundary-window event, never a silent misprediction, and
re-learning over the full horizon leaves every decision key
single-valued (zero conflicts across $3{,}956$ segments).
The sharpest check runs the machine itself on the calculus's own
\emph{predictions}: configurations forecast up to $139$ sweeps
beyond any simulated history, written onto a literal tape and
handed to the raw machine, reproduce every forecast exactly, step
counts included. This is predictive emulation (certify the law, then
verify forecasts) standing, for the first time in this program,
on a state space of growing dimension. The cohort boundary is
sharp on both sides: a tenfold budget increase changes no verdict
(all fourteen ranked candidates that failed the exact-spec gate
still fail it, so the eleven-machine array class is complete under
this criterion), and porting the formalism without machine-specific
rules across the other ten machines places one at a $99.8\%$
architecture match with $82.4\%$ of held-out segments reproduced
from literal rules alone, and two more at $99.3\%$ and $98.6\%$
exactness where extraction succeeds.

The classification also carries a quantitative signature. The
abstract backward cone of dossier-3, the tree of renewal-level
states dying within $k$ steps, has level counts
$9, 19, 31, 50, 81, \ldots$ satisfying $L(k) = L(k{-}1) + L(k{-}2)$
\emph{exactly} from depth two on: a Fibonacci tree, branching at the
golden ratio, and the same $\varphi \approx 1.618$ appears
independently as the forward factor complexity. The mechanism is
visible: a terminate step flips a parity, so no branch word contains
two consecutive terminates, precisely the constraint that
generates Fibonacci counting. The standing, falsifiable prediction
for the array class: each machine's death tree satisfies a small
integer recurrence with a Pisot-like constant determined by its
episode-alternation rules, the golden ratio being the founding case.
The rank-1 machine supplies the second data point. Its cycle
grammar inverts in closed form: the number of one-sweep preimages
of an interior word equals $\prod_i 2^{\max(0,\,t_i-2)}$ over its
$(1,3)$-train lengths $t_i$, so this death tree branches 2-adically
in train lengths where dossier-3's branches at $\varphi$.

\section{The verification spectrum: where this sits, and the goal}
This work sits on a spectrum of what ``verifying a machine'' can
mean; both endpoints of the spectrum are now theorems.

\emph{Starting verification} is the ground-up kind: replay and check
every step. The Lean development here is this, industrialized: the
kernel checks each of the $10^{4}$ startup steps individually, and the
trust surface is two axioms and a proof checker. It is complete and
unconditional, but it can never outrun the machine: verifying $n$
steps costs $n$.

\emph{Predictive emulation} is the middle of the spectrum and the
substance of this paper. Instead of replaying steps, certify the
machine's \emph{law}, the episode theorems and the five-integer
map, and then verify \emph{forecasts}: where the next probe event
can occur, what the configuration will be $10^{6}$ steps from now,
whether halting is reachable from the current shape. One certified
episode theorem covers unboundedly many raw steps; the certified
recurrence covers all of them. Verification cost decouples from
runtime. This is what lets two machines' entire futures be checked
against one explicit arithmetic conjecture.

\emph{Ending verification} is the horizon, taken here as the
program's goal: $\mathsf{MIP}^{*} = \mathsf{RE}$
\cite{mipre}. A classical polynomial-time verifier, interacting with
two entangled provers, can verify \emph{any} recursively enumerable
statement, with the provers, not the verifier, bearing the
unbounded cost; in the authors' words, ``there is an efficient
reduction from the Halting Problem to the problem of deciding
whether a two-player nonlocal game has entangled value 1 or at most
1/2'' \cite{mipre}. Non-halting statements such as ours live one level up
(they are $\Pi_{1}$; zero-gap variants of $\mathsf{MIP}^{*}$ reach
$\Pi_{2}^{0}$ \cite{mnyu}), so the entangled-prover frame in
principle spans exactly the class of claims the busy beaver program
generates.

This mapping is not only a framing; the certified reduction plays as
an actual game. Define $G(K, r)$, verifying the claim ``the machine
halts within $K$ renewals'': two isolated provers commit to a
history $(s_0, \ldots, s_K)$ of renewal states and a halt index
$k^*$; the verifier checks the seed, samples $r$ indices $j$ and
requires cross-consistency of the provers' snapshots together with
$R(s_j) = s_{j+1}$ (one constant-cost arithmetic check each, which
the certified theorems entitle in place of replay), and requires
$s_{k^*}$ to lie in the fatal set, whose members provably halt
within about fifty raw steps. A halting machine gives honest provers
a probability-1 win. Since $R$ is deterministic with fixed seed, the
only law-consistent history is the true orbit, which stays at
distance at least 4 above the fatal window over the tested horizon,
so any winning strategy needs a seam, caught per sample with
probability $1/K$. Played at $K = 10^5$: the true orbit contains
zero fatal states, so no false acceptance is possible on the real
history; spliced forgeries are accepted at the rate the seam bound
predicts (0.998 at $r = 64$, 0.745 at $r = 2^{14}$, 0 in all trials
at $r = 2^{18}$ and $2^{20}$, where the analytic bound is below
$3 \times 10^{-5}$). The verifier's total work is $r$ integer
checks on five-integer states whose raw-tape realizations at that
depth are astronomically long: verification cost decoupled from
runtime, played as a game. The same game runs unchanged over the
array-class word calculus, with one rewrite-system evaluation as the
law check and raw execution of the materialized word as the terminal
check; forged terminals built from the machine's own depth-24 halt
cone (stamped into true orbit words of about $10^4$ cells) genuinely
halt, and are then caught by law sampling at the identical seam
rate, 0 acceptances in all trials at $r \ge 2048$ over a
346-segment word history.

The alignment is this: kernel verification anchors
truth at the bottom; predictive emulation compresses it in the middle;
and $\mathsf{MIP}^{*} = \mathsf{RE}$ says that at the top,
verification of halting-type claims is possible \emph{in principle}
with entanglement as the resource. The quantum-inspired toolkit
above
(reversible vector maps, state-space geometry) borrows disciplines
from that endpoint; the open engineering-of-mathematics question is
how much of the middle rung, certified forecasts, can be pushed
toward it, replacing replay with interaction. Nothing in the
results
depends on this framing; it states the destination the pipeline is
pointed at.

\section{A benchmark for the theory of learning}
There are three reasons this may interest this community. The first
is the workflow. This project is an instance of
the emerging human--AI--kernel pattern in mathematics, in which AI
systems draft and compile proofs at scale, a proof kernel
arbitrates, and humans own claims; this is the pattern of Tao's
Equational Theories Project, which ``successfully determined all
22\,028\,942 edges of the implication graph between the 4694
simplest equational laws on magmas,'' and of the new Palomar
registry for Lean-verified results \cite{etp,palomar}. All
of the Lean code was drafted by an AI assistant from validated
empirical
specifications; the kernel checked roughly fifteen thousand lines; the
generators are convenience, not trust surface. The second reason is
methodological kinship. Finding that a high-dimensional trajectory is
governed by a low-dimensional piecewise-affine law, by choosing
coordinates in which the structure is linear, is the daily business of
representation learning. Here the same move is carried one step
further: the discovered representation is not evaluated on held-out
data but proven exactly correct, step by step, against the system
that generated it. Each pipeline stage has a standard learning-theory
name. Event extraction is feature engineering; the coordinate charts
are the representation; the piecewise-affine law is a linear model in
the right representation; fail-closed validation is a generalization
test with a zero-tolerance loss; the kernel proof replaces the test
set.

The third reason is a concrete offer. Busy beaver machines generate
unbounded, exactly labeled, law-governed sequence data, and the
census grades them: fixed-dimension machines whose ground-truth
representation is a proven five-integer state, array-class machines
whose ground truth is a word-rewrite system of growing dimension,
and a structured remainder with fitted regime laws. A network
trained to emulate one of these machines can be scored not against
samples but against certainty: slot it in as prover A of the halting
game and every forecast it makes is checked at constant cost, with
any deviation from the certified law caught at the seam rate. What a
feature learner should discover here is known exactly, chart by
chart, which is precisely the situation the mathematical theory of
deep networks lacks in natural data.

\paragraph{Scope.} No unconditional non-halting claim is made. The
coordinate charts guide discovery and exposition; no theorem depends on
them. Empirical horizons are what was run, not what is true.
